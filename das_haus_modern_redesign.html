The House

A House With More Than a Century of Stories.

Fourteen rooms, one very long kitchen table, a library that has survived every renovation, and a garden that turns into a second living room every summer.

Our Home Since 1901

Old Walls, Current Life.

The building has been ours since the turn of the last century, and every generation has left something behind: a bookshelf built into a wall, a repaired staircase, a tradition nobody can quite date. We keep the parts that still work and modernise the rest — new wiring, fibre in every room, a kitchen built for people who actually cook.

What has not changed is the way the house is used. It is a place to study in the afternoon and to argue about something unimportant until late at night, and the two rarely get in each other's way.

The Rooms We Share

Beyond the private rooms, the house is built around spaces that belong to everyone. This is where most of what people remember later actually happened.

The Kneipsaal

The largest room in the house and the reason it was built this way: dark panelling, a long table, and enough space for eighty people to sit down together. It hosts our formal evenings, our loudest ones, and — on quiet weekday afternoons — a surprising amount of exam preparation.

menu_book

The Library

Silent by agreement, not by rule. Shelves that have grown for a hundred years, six desks, good lamps, and the unspoken understanding that nobody takes a phone call in here.

deck

Garden & Terrace

From May onwards, most of the house simply moves outside. There is a grill, a stack of mismatched chairs, and an old lime tree that keeps the terrace usable even in August.

The Kitchen

Two ovens, far too many pots, and a table long enough that nobody eats alone. Cooking here is a rota, not a chore — and the best conversations of the week tend to happen while somebody is doing the dishes.
